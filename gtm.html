GTM Strategy 2.0

🎯 What PulseDNA Actually Sells

PulseDNA is not federated ML tooling. PulseDNA is an execution fabric with three repeatable revenue levers: infrastructure usage (GPU-hour billing + tokenized inference), software licensing (platform + compliance modules), and expert verification services (human-in-the-loop audit and submission support).

This is infrastructure software, not consulting. No long-tail customization. No staffing model. No bespoke one-off solutions. Government work is product-aligned and fixed-scope — learnings flow back into the commercial platform.

GTM Motion — Three Lanes
Lane 1 — Bottom-Up Pilots

Entry via Magic Lens at $50K–$200K/yr. Rapid value in days to weeks. No infrastructure commitment required. Sandbox or limited evaluation environment. Focus: regulatory compliance acceleration. Land in one department, expand across the facility.


→ pulsednafederatedai.com/magiclens
Lane 2 — Mid-Market Compliance

$250K–$750K/yr regulated enterprise contracts. Targets compliance officers, legal teams, and regulatory affairs leads in nuclear, clinical, and financial services. Co-sell motion with Supermicro for hardware-adjacent deals. John Snow Labs partnership for unstructured clinical data (hand-written notes, NLP).

Lane 3 — Sovereign Infrastructure

PulseFINE™ full infrastructure build at $5M–$15M per engagement. Air-gapped deployment. Full operational sovereignty. Targets nuclear operators, national labs, and classified government programs. Months for pilot → fully air-gapped production. Multi-year lifecycle contracts.

Government as Credibility Flywheel

Government engagements generate third-party credibility, compliance artifacts, and procurement-grade references that materially de-risk enterprise adoption in law, finance, and healthcare. SAM.GOV registered. SBA certified. CAGE: 0YRQ3. Government work is product-aligned — not a growth crutch.

🔮 Why Hyperscalers Become Optional, Not Competitors
PulseDNA sits in the same infrastructure generation as Neysa (AI compute infrastructure), Zyphra (model-native deployment), and General Intuition (hardware-aware AI) — but with one critical difference: federated, sovereign-first execution is native, not bolted on. That's why hyperscalers become optional infrastructure that PulseDNA can route through — not competitors that PulseDNA has to displace.
